Day 1Tue, Nov 10 · Gwanak-gu

Land, settle, steam the flight out

jjimjilbangoptional shakeoutfree afternoon
  • 07:15morning
    free ICN → AREX to Seoul Stn → Line 2 to Sillim/SNU. Store bags at the hotel (check-in 15:00).Get a T-money card at the airport station before you tap in.
  • 09:30brunch
    free First real meal near SNU — kalguksu (knife-cut noodles) or gimbap; everything's cheap and open early around the university.
  • 11:00nap
    Seriously. Red-eye + 2 nights of no sleep ruins the rest of the trip. Nothing is scheduled until the evening.
  • 16:30shakeout
    free Optional 4 km jog on the Anyangcheon stream path behind the hotel — flat, lit, zero thinking. Skip it guilt-free.
  • 19:00evening
    🎟 day-of Sillim jjimjilbang (Korean bathhouse) — hot rooms, cold plunge, iced sikhye on a heated floor. 2–3 h. This is the single best jet-lag cure there is. Just pick the highest-rated 찜질방 near Sillim Station on Naver Map and walk in.
Day 2Wed, Nov 11 · Gwanaksan + Sinchon

The mountain behind the hotel

hikearcheryfree afternoon
  • 08:00anchor
    free Gwanaksan, Sadeokgol trail → Yeongbong peak (629 m) → down the SNU side. ~3 h round trip, trailhead 10 min from the hotel. Peak fall color week. Take the 300 won "Sadeokgol bus" up the valley road if your legs say no.
  • 12:30lunch
    free Hearty lunch at the SNU back gate — student-priced Korean comfort food. Then rest; you earned it.
  • 16:30afternoon
    Free. Wander Sinchon / Ewha if you want people-watching; stay in if you don't.
  • 19:00evening
    🎟 day-of Archery at Robin Hood Archery, Sinchon — 15 min from the hotel. Walk-in, ~₩15–20k/hour, staff show you the form in 2 minutes. Korea is the Olympic archery superpower; the cafe gear is genuinely nice. Go after dinner — lanes open late.
Day 3Thu, Nov 12 · Jongno

Palace day — Secret Garden + old city

palacesmarket foodfree evening
  • 09:30anchor
    🔒 advance Changdeokgung Palace + Huwon (Secret Garden) timed guided tour — autumn foliage at its absolute peak inside the garden. Booking guide (the 10:00 / 6-day rule). Tickets go live 6 days before at 10:00 KST and the online allotment sells out in minutes. Set an alarm for Thu Nov 6, 09:55 KST. Backup: 50 same-day tickets sell at the gate — arrive before opening.
  • 12:30lunch
    🎟 day-of Gwangjang Market, 10 min walk — bindaetteok (mung-bean pancakes) + yukhoe (Korean beef tartare) at the food alleys. Point at whatever has the longest line.
  • 14:30afternoon
    🎟 day-of Walk off lunch: Bukchon Hanok Village rooftops → Ikseon-dong alley cafés. Optional detour: rent a hanbok (walk-in, ₩15–25k/2h) — it also gets you free palace entry.
  • 18:30evening
    Free. Insadong tea houses, or metro home early — tomorrow is an early call.
Day 4Fri, Nov 13 · DMZ (Paju)

DMZ day — the strangest border on earth

DMZ/JSAeasy evening
  • 07:00anchor
    🔒 advance Full-day DMZ tour (Third Tunnel → Dora Observatory → Dorasan Station; JSA/Panmunjom add-on if you want the blue huts). Hotel pickup, back ~15:00–16:00. How to book, JSA vs standard · operators: Koridoor, Klook. Government clearance is required — you can't just show up. Book at least 3–4 days ahead (ASAP is better; JSA slots sell first) and bring your passport. Dress code: no ripped jeans/sandals for JSA.
  • 16:30afternoon
    Back in town, mellow. The tour day is longer than it sounds.
  • 19:00evening
    🎟 day-of Comfort dinner in Euljiro / Sewoon Sangga — old-electronics district that turns into a rooftop-bar zone at night. Or just jjimjilbang round two.
Day 5Sat, Nov 14 · courts + skyline

Tennis, then the whole city from above

tennisobservatoryshow
  • 09:00anchor
    🔒 advance Tennis on a public court — book via Seoul yeyak (court finder: seoultennis.co.kr). ~₩10–20k/hour, floodlit public courts are common. Weekend morning slots vanish fast — grab one the moment they open (a few days ahead; the page shows the exact window). Bad weather? Move this to Sun and take the indoor club option near Sillim.
  • 12:30lunch
    Free. Jamsil side if you're near the courts.
  • 15:30afternoon
    🎟 day-of Seoul Sky at Lotte World Tower (555 m) — time it so you go up in daylight and come down in the dark. Glass-floored Sky Deck included. Timed tickets, easy to buy same-day online or at the door on a Friday. ₩29k-ish.
  • 20:00evening
    🔒 advance NANTA at the Myeongdong theatre — 90 min of knife-and-drum kitchen comedy, zero words, still hilarious. Official tickets. Online sale opens ~20 days out (so ~Oct 26). Weekend evening shows sell; a weekday seat is easier. 5 min walk from Myeongdong station exit 6.

runcycling (opt.)free afternoon

    🎟 day-of Swap the run for a Han River bike ride: Ttukseom rentals (₩3–5k/h). It's a "slower legs" weekend — your call.

  • 18:00evening
    🎟 day-of Flex evening. Options that all take walk-ins: Hongdae street food + live buskers · PC bang (Korean esports PC café — ₩1–2k/h, absurd hardware) · Real Shot archery/shooting zone · noraebang (private karaoke, ₩15k/h). Or start packing.

Swaps & flex slots — the "maybe" pile
  • Bukhansan, Baegundae (836 m) — the big one, ~4–5 h from Bukhansanseong trailhead. Slot it in place of the Sun run or the Sat afternoon if the forecast is perfect. Free, unbooked. Start by 8.
  • K-pop dance class (Hongdae studios, ~₩30k, book same-week) — a genuinely fun 90 min even if you can't dance.
  • Gwangjang Market cooking class — book ~1 week out if the idea grabs you; otherwise the market eating is the main event anyway.
  • Gyeongbokgung night viewing — runs in limited windows and needs advance tickets; check Visit Seoul for Nov dates. If it's on, it beats any bar in the city.
  • Batting cages — round-the-clock chains everywhere; 3 min of chaos for ₩2k.
Why this shape
Only two real "sport" anchors (tennis + the river run) and two hikes — the rest of the week is events, food, and deliberate blank space. Jjimjilbang on arrival night fixes jet lag better than any activity. The two advance bookings (Secret Garden, DMZ) are the two things in Seoul that genuinely can't be improvised. Archery, observatory, shows, markets, PC bangs: all walk-in. Days start early-ish (Korea is a morning country) and the afternoons are intentionally empty — that's when the good wandering happens.
