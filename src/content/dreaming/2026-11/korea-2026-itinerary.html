Spa, skin, or scalpel?

Real Seoul venues and current prices. The short version: do the spa, do a light facial if you want, and do not get a birthmark excised on this trip.

My call: jjimjilbang on arrival night, a proper hot-spa day mid-trip, a no-downtime facial on Day 4, and a consultation only for the birthmark. An excision is a minor surgery with a 1–3 week healing window and a pathology result — it doesn't belong between a red-eye and a return flight.

Why not excise a flat birthmark here? A truly flat, evenly coloured birthmark (café-au-lait, a flat congenital nevus) is usually a pigment problem, not a lump. The first-line treatment is laser over multiple sessions — not a knife. Excision removes full thickness, leaves a linear scar, and is the right move when a lesion is raised, changing, asymmetric, multi-coloured, bleeding, or needs pathology. That's a dermatologist's decision, not a walk-in beauty treatment. If it is excised, you can't soak in a bath or hot spring while it heals, and you may need suture removal 5–14 days later — i.e. after you're home.

If you do want the birthmark looked at properly: book a dermatologist, not an aesthetic “mole removal” counter. Ask three questions: (1) Is this lesion suitable for laser, or does it need a biopsy/excision? (2) Will the tissue go to pathology, and how do I get the English report? (3) What is the wound-care and suture-removal plan if I fly home? If the answer to (1) is “excision,” the answer to “should I do it on this trip?” is no.

  • Severance Hospital — 50-1 Yonsei-ro, Seodaemun-gu · International Health Care Center — central, English support, emergency department.
  • Samsung Medical Center — 81 Irwon-ro, Gangnam-gu · International Clinic — another English-friendly tertiary option.
  • Gangnam Nanoori Hospital — 731 Eonju-ro, Gangnam-gu · Hakdong Station Exit 10 · English WhatsApp +82-10-6290-9796 — foot & ankle orthopedics with English coordinators; good if you want a same-week ortho opinion.

Go same-day if the toe is hot, red, spreading, weeping, or you have a fever. Don't soak an open nail bed, and don't let a spa visit be the reason you ignore it.

The toe, specifically — partial nail avulsion
  • What you're managing: part of the nail lifted off the nail bed. Minor, but the bed stays raw and pressure-sensitive for a few days, and the nail takes weeks to regrow. By Day 1 you're roughly 8 days out.
  • Daily care: saline rinse, pat dry, non-stick dressing. A silicone toe cap, or a roomy shoe with the laces loosened over the toe box. Only trim a snagging nail edge if a clinician tells you to.
  • Sauna: hot rooms are fine once the surface is closed and not weeping. Skip shared baths and pools while the nail bed is raw — that includes the Aquafield baths and rooftop pool. Dry the toe first, then the rest.
  • Tennis and running: fine if it's pain-free in shoes. The risk is pivoting and shoe pressure, not impact. Screen tennis is the right version; a short flat run is fine, a mountain is not.
  • Downhill is the tell: toes jam into the front of the shoe on descents. If Namsan hurts going down, turn around. Toe cap on, laces loose.
  • Same-day clinic if: spreading redness, heat, pus, fever, or pain that's getting worse instead of better.

Group hike options (English)
  • Seoul Easy Hiking — free. 900+ members, weekend easy walks, English-speaking host. The Sunday Nov 15 Namsan walk is 2 h at an easy pace and exists to meet people. RSVP on Meetup; it's the lowest-risk social option on this trip.
  • Trazy — Ansan Mountain hike + jjimjilbang, ~US$58. English guide, 10:00–15:00, meet at Dongnimmun Station Exit 5. Small group (2–10), every day except Tuesday, ends at Sinchon or Seoul Station.   • Seoul Hiking Nature — the bigger Meetup/Facebook group. More events, but many are 3–5 h and faster. Read the distance and pace before joining.
  • Adventure Korea / WinK — weekend coach trips, very social, long days. Better for a future trip than a healing toe.
If the toe is better than expected
  • Add a second 30-min screen-tennis block on Day 6 — same court, same booking.
  • Book the Trazy Ansan half-day as the step up from the Namsan walk — guided, English, and it ends in a jjimjilbang.
  • A short flat run on the paved Anyangcheon path — 20–30 min, not the day before travel. Your doctor already cleared running; keep it boring and flat.
  • Add a flat 30 min walk along the Anyangcheon stream behind the hotel — paved, lit, no hills.
  • Swap the Seoul Forest loop for a longer flat Han River path at Yeouido, but keep it under an hour and stop at the first twinge.
  • Do not add Gwanaksan, Bukhansan, outdoor tennis, or a run. "Better" is not "healed."
If the toe is worse than expected
  • Day 2 is already the clinic. If it's worse before then, go Day 1 instead and move the bathhouse to Day 5.
  • Cut Day 3 (palace) and Day 6 (tennis and Namsan). Keep Day 2 (clinic + museum) and Day 5 (spa) — both are sit-down days.
  • Taxi everywhere, including the 8-min walk to the bathhouse.
  • Skip the baths entirely if the nail bed is raw or weeping, and see a doctor same-day if it's hot, red, spreading, or oozing.
